The settings that decide how a document sits on your screen. All of them are in the View menu, and Clumsy remembers the last one you picked.

## Themes

The palette button near the right end of the toolbar changes the colors of Clumsy itself: the toolbar, tabs, sidebar, and sheets. Point at a theme in its menu to see it, and click to keep it. The same nine choices are in View > Theme.

- **Match System** is the default. Clumsy is light when your computer is light, and Ember when it's dark.
- **Clumsy Light** is that light look, pinned. **Clumsy Dark** is the neutral gray and blue dark look Clumsy had before themes.
- **Ember**, **Ice**, and **Forest** are dark, with an orange, blue, or green accent.
- **Paper**, **Snow**, and **Bloom** are light: warm off-white, cool white, and rose.

From the keyboard, open the Command Palette with [[⇧⌘P]], type `theme`, and move through the list with the arrow keys. Each one shows as you land on it. [[Return]] keeps it and [[Esc]] puts back what you had.

The choice covers every window and is still there the next time you open Clumsy.

A theme never recolors your document. Pages, highlights, shapes, stamps, comparison colors, and find matches look the same in all nine, and printing and exporting don't change. Dark pages, below, is the setting that changes how a page looks, and it's separate: until you turn it on or off yourself, it follows the theme, so dark themes start with dark pages.

## Dark pages

[[⇧⌘D]] turns the page dark: white paper becomes near-black and black text becomes light.

The usual way to do that makes every photo look like a film negative. Clumsy finds the images on each page and draws them back the way they were, so text is inverted and pictures aren't. Thumbnails and the Document Map do the same.

A scanned page is one picture of paper, so Clumsy treats it as a page, not a photo, and turns it dark with the rest. A full-page photo stays as it is.

If a document's other "images" are really line drawings or pieces of a scan, you may want them inverted too. The toolbar has a menu for it with two choices, **Keep images** and **Invert images**, or use View > Invert Images in Dark Pages.

Dark pages change what you see, not the file. Printing and saving are unaffected.

## Zoom

| Do this | With this |
| --- | --- |
| Zoom in and out | [[⌘=]] and [[⌘-]] |
| Actual Size | [[⌘0]] |
| Fit Width | [[⌘9]] |
| Fit Page | [[⌥⌘9]] |

Fit Width and Fit Page stick: resize the window, open the sidebar, or split the pane, and the page re-fits itself. The zoom menu in the toolbar also has fixed percentages from 50% to 400%.

## Scrolling and spreads

View has four ways to scroll:

- **Vertical Scrolling** is the default: one continuous column.
- **Horizontal Scrolling** lays pages left to right. Good for slides.
- **Wrapped Scrolling** fills the window with as many pages across as fit. Zoom out and it becomes a contact sheet.
- **Page Scrolling** shows one page, or one spread, at a time.

And three spread settings: **No Spreads**, **Odd Spreads**, and **Even Spreads**. Spreads put two pages side by side like an open book. Odd starts the pairs at page 1. Even leaves page 1 alone as a cover, which is what most books and magazines want.

The first time you open a file, Clumsy uses the layout the document asks for, if it asks. After that it remembers yours.

## Rotating the view

[[⌘R]] and [[⌘L]] turn the view a quarter turn. This is only how you're looking at it and isn't saved. To fix a sideways page for good, use Pages > Rotate Pages, covered in [Rearranging pages](/guides/page-edits).

## The Hand tool

Hold [[Space]] and drag to pan the page, from any tool. Let go and you're back to what you were doing. Tools > Hand keeps it on.

A quick tap of [[Space]] scrolls down one screen. [[⇧Space]] scrolls back up.

## Caret Browsing

[[F7]] puts a text cursor in the page so you can select text from the keyboard. Click some text to place it, then:

- Arrow keys move by character and by line. Up and Down carry on to the next page.
- [[⌥←]] and [[⌥→]] move by word.
- [[Home]] and [[End]] go to the ends of the line.
- Hold [[⇧]] with any of those to select.

Then [[⌘C]] to copy, or highlight the selection from the Tools menu.
